            <div class="case-study-header">

                <h1> FMEA</h1>

            </div>

                <div class="case-study-summary">
                    <div class="case-study-summary__img">
                            <a href="https://asq.org/quality-progress/articles/fmea-minus-the-pain?id=d942e78095e845bba538ece95781fdaa" target="_blank">
                                <img src="/-/media/Images/LAQ/FMEA-ASQ-Featured.png" alt="Partial FMEA Table"></a>
                    </div>


                        <h3 class="case-study__subtitle">Overview</h3>
                        <p></p><p>Also called: potential failure mode and effects analysis; failure modes, effects and criticality analysis (FMECA)</p>
<p>Failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA), developed by the U.S. military in the 1940s, is a systematic, step-by-step approach to identify and prioritize possible failures in a design, manufacturing or assembly process, product, or service. It is a common <a href="https://asq.org/quality-resources/process-analysis-tools" target="_blank">risk analysis tool</a>. The goal of this proactive tool is to mitigate or eliminate potential failures.</p>
<ul class="rte-unordered-list"><li><strong>"Failure mode"</strong> means the way, or mode, in which something might fail. Failures are any errors or defects, especially those that affect the customer, and can be potential or actual.</li>
    <li><strong>"Effects analysis"</strong> refers to studying the consequences of those failures.</li>
</ul><p>Failures are prioritized according to how serious their consequences are, how frequently they occur, and how easily they can be detected. The purpose of FMEA is to take actions to eliminate, reduce, and/or mitigate failures, starting with those deemed highest priority.</p>
<p>FMEA also documents current knowledge and actions about the risks of failures to use for continuous improvement efforts. FMEA can be used during design (design FMEA, or DFMEA) to prevent failures. Later, it can be used for process control (process FMEA, or PFMEA), as well as before and during ongoing operations. Ideally, FMEA begins during the earliest conceptual stages of design and continues throughout the life of the product or service. FMEA has bigger leverage and impact in the early stages of development when changes are less costly to implement.</p>
<div class="btn orange"><a rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://asq.org/quality-resources/quality-glossary/f" target="_blank">Quality Glossary Definition</a></div>
                </div>


        </div>
        <div class="col-xs-12 col-sm-12 col-md-4 col-lg-3 col-lg-offset-1">
                <a href="https://asq.org/training/fmea-for-beginners-fmea01jpr" target="_blank">
                    <img src="/-/media/Images/LAQ/CTA-TopRight/400x533_ASQ-LAQ-ELearning-FMEA.jpg" class="member-gift-image" alt="Image promoting the ASQ course for learning FMEA for beginners."></a>
        </div>

<div class="content-section">
    
    <div id="laq-accordion-container">

        <div id="laq-information" style="">
            <div class="tab-block js-tab-block tab-block-accordion">
                    <section class="tab-block__pane"><header class="tab-block__pane-title clearfix"><a class="tab-block__pane-link js-match-height js-content-toggle" href="#tab_1">WHEN TO USE FMEA</a>
                        </header><div id="tab_1" class="tab-block__pane-content">
                            <div class="row">
                                <div class="col-sm-12 col-md-8">
                                    <h3>WHEN TO USE FMEA</h3>
                                        <p></p><p>FMEA is used when a process, product, or service is being designed, redesigned, or evaluated. It also is used:</p>
<ul class="rte-unordered-list"><li>After <a href="https://asq.org/quality-resources/qfd-quality-function-deployment" target="_blank">quality function deployment (QFD)</a></li>
    <li>When an existing process, product, or service is being applied in a new way</li>
    <li>Before developing control plans for a new or modified process</li>
    <li>When improvement goals are planned for an existing process, product, or service</li>
    <li>When analyzing failures of an existing process, product, or service</li>
</ul></div>
                                <div class="col-md-4">
                                        <a href="https://asq.org/quality-progress/articles/a-fundamental-fmea-flaw?id=59147386ea0b44c28207888c7e804e6d" target="_blank"><img class="member-gift-image" src="/-/media/Images/LAQ/FMEA-ASQ-1.png" alt="Sample FMEA Form"></a>
                                </div>
                            </div>
                        </div>
                    </section><section class="tab-block__pane"><header class="tab-block__pane-title clearfix"><a class="tab-block__pane-link js-match-height js-content-toggle" href="#tab_2">FMEA: STEP BY STEP</a>
                        </header><div id="tab_2" class="tab-block__pane-content">
                            <div class="row">
                                <div class="col-sm-12 col-md-8">
                                    <h3>FMEA: STEP BY STEP</h3>
                                        <p></p><p>There are several steps in the FMEA process.</p>
<ol><li><strong>Build a team:</strong> Assemble a multidisciplinary, cross-functional <a rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://asq.org/quality-resources/teams" target="_blank">team</a> of people with diverse knowledge about the process, product, or service, as well as customer needs. The team usually consists of representatives from design, manufacturing, quality, testing, reliability, maintenance, purchasing (and suppliers), sales, marketing (and customers), and customer service.</li>
    <li><strong>Set the scope:</strong> Define the FMEA’s scope. Is it for concept, system, design, process, or service? What are the boundaries? How detailed should the scope be? Use <a rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://asq.org/quality-resources/flowchart" target="_blank">flowcharts</a> to identify the scope and make sure every team member understands it in detail.</li>
    <li><strong>Identify information:</strong> Fill in the identifying information at the top of your <a rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://asq.org/-/media/public/learn-about-quality/data-collection-analysis-tools/asq-fmea-template.xls" target="_blank">FMEA form</a>. The remaining steps look to uncover information that will go into the columns of the form.</li>
    <li><strong>Set scope functions:</strong> Identify the functions of your scope: "What is the purpose of this system, design, process, or service? What do our customers expect it to do?" Name it with a verb followed by a noun. Usually, you can break the scope into separate subsystems, items, parts, assemblies, or process steps and identify the function of each.</li>
    <li><strong>Identify failure potential:</strong> For each function, identify the ways failure could happen. Brainstorm. These are potential failure modes. This is the most important activity in FMEA. If necessary, rewrite the function with more detail to ensure the failure modes show a loss of that function.</li>
    <li><strong>Identify consequences:</strong> For each failure mode, identify the consequences on the system, related systems, process, related processes, product, service, customer, or regulations. These are potential failure effects. Ask, "What does the customer experience because of this failure? What happens when this failure occurs?"</li>
    <li><strong>Rate severity:</strong> Determine how serious each effect is. This is the severity (S) rating. Severity usually is rated on a scale from one to 10: One is insignificant and 10 is catastrophic. If a failure mode has more than one effect, write on the FMEA form only the highest severity rating for that failure mode.</li>
    <li><strong>Determine root causes: </strong>For each failure mode, determine all the potential root causes. Use tools classified as <a rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://asq.org/quality-resources/root-cause-analysis" target="_blank&gt;cause analysis tools&lt;/a&gt;, as well as the best knowledge and experience of the team. List all possible causes for each failure mode on the FMEA form.&lt;/li&gt;
    &lt;li&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Assign occurrence:&lt;/strong&gt; For each cause, determine the occurrence (O) rating. This rating estimates the probability of failure occurring for that reason during the lifetime of your scope. Usually, occurrence is rated on a scale from one to 10: One is extremely unlikely and 10 is inevitable. On the FMEA form, list the occurrence rating for each cause.&lt;/li&gt;
    &lt;li&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Identify process controls:&lt;/strong&gt; For each cause, identify current process controls. These are tests, procedures, or mechanisms that you now have in place to keep failures from reaching the customer. These controls might prevent the cause from happening, reduce the likelihood it will happen, or detect failure &lt;em&gt;after &lt;/em&gt;the cause already has happened but &lt;em&gt;before &lt;/em&gt;the customer is affected.&lt;/li&gt;
    &lt;li&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Determine detection:&lt;/strong&gt; For each control, determine the detection (D) rating. This rating estimates how well the controls can detect either the cause or its failure mode after they have happened but before the customer is affected. Usually, detection is usually rated on a scale from one to 10: One means the control absolutely is certain to detect the problem and 10 means the control is certain not to detect the problem (or no control exists). On the FMEA form, list the detection rating for each cause.&lt;/li&gt;
    &lt;li&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Calculate the risk priority number (RPN):&lt;/strong&gt; Calculate the RPN, which equals S × O × D. These numbers provide guidance for ranking potential failures in the sequence in which they should be addressed. Usually, an organization will establish a threshold (for example, 100 or 125). When the RPN exceeds the threshold, actions are required. Some practices also require actions when severity is high at nine or 10.&lt;/li&gt;
    &lt;li&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Recommend action:&lt;/strong&gt; Identify recommended actions for the RPN exceeding the threshold. These actions may be design or process changes to lower severity or occurrence. They may be additional controls to improve detection. Also note who is responsible for the actions and target completion dates.&lt;/li&gt;
    &lt;li&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Keep notes:&lt;/strong&gt; As actions are completed, note results and the date on the FMEA form. Also, note new S, O, or D ratings as well as new RPNs. The post-action RPN must be below the threshold, otherwise additional actions are required until the RPN is below the threshold.&lt;/li&gt;
    &lt;li&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Re-evaluate:&lt;/strong&gt; Re-evaluate the FMEA periodically to ensure the implemented actions are effective, and to identify any new potential failure modes that may have arisen.&lt;/li&gt;
    &lt;/ol&gt;
    &lt;p&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Note:&lt;/strong&gt; This is a general procedure. Specific details may vary with standards of your organization or industry. Before undertaking an FMEA process, learn more about standards and specific methods in your organization and industry through other references and training. ASQ members may make use of an &lt;a href=" failure-modes-and-effects-analysis-template?id="95ce876954b64b60892cbcca2d4525d1">FMEA Template</a> and <a rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://asq.org/quality-resources/benchmarking/how-to-use-the-failure-modes-and-effects-analysis-template?id=e82d06690dfd4c6f9ab661f27bd8e3d8" target="_blank">How-To-Use documentation</a> to aid in these efforts.
    <p> </p>
    </li>
</ol></div>

                                    <h3>FMEA EXAMPLE</h3>
                                        <p></p><p>A manufacturer performed a process FMEA on its tablet packaging process. The included figure shows part of it. As shown, two of the process steps have four potential failure modes that all incurred high RPNs (&gt; 125). Sufficient actions were identified and taken that lowered all RPNs to an acceptable level (&lt; 125). Essentially, this is what FMEA is trying to accomplish.</p>

                        </header><div id="tab_4" class="tab-block__pane-content">
                            <div class="row">
                                <div class="col-sm-12 col-md-8">
                                    <h3>FMEA LIMITATIONS</h3>
                                        <p></p><p>The FMEA method does have some shortfalls. The one-size-fits-all format can be inefficient, for example, which leads to ineffectiveness. Lack of return on investment (ROI) assessment over actions can amplify the deficiency. In many cases, a lack of data also amplify the deficiency, making the three-dimensioned risk assessment difficult and unreliable, which erodes ROI. </p>
<p>Even with these challenges, FMEA still is a powerful method for identifying and mitigating potential risks in systems, processes, and designs, ultimately leading to improved reliability, safety, and quality.</p>
<p>One approach to counteract these issues is to follow a simpler risk analysis and assessment, as pictured.</p>
