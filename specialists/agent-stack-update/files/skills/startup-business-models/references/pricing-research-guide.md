# Pricing Research Guide

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer, in order: actual purchase/renewal behaviour; controlled pricing tests; negotiated deal data; lost-deal/discount data; structured customer interviews; competitor pages; generic benchmarks.

## Interview focus

Ask about current alternative, consequence/cost of the problem, budget ownership, purchase trigger, value metric, usage variability, switching friction, and what makes a price feel unfair or risky. Avoid asking only "what would you pay?"

## Research outputs

- segments and jobs-to-be-done;
- candidate value metric;
- willingness-to-pay evidence/range;
- packaging boundaries and upgrade triggers;
- competitive anchors;
- objections and discount pressure;
- hypotheses that require live testing.

Use `pricing-strategy` for the final pricing/packaging decision and `financial-unit-economics` to validate economic consequences.
